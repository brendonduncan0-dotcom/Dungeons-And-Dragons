## Premise

For two months, the village of Thistlewick has paid monthly tribute to "Vhalzareth the Undying," an ancient red dragon that supposedly lairs in the nearby hills. In truth, "Vhalzareth" is Nix — a runaway red dragon wyrmling who found a Ring of Minor Illusions and turned it into the con of his short life. The guild is hired to end the tribute racket, but the party's investigation unravels into something messier than a monster hunt: a scared kid playing dress-up as a monster, a village that's been paying protection money to a con artist, and — waiting in the wings — a very real, very furious mother dragon who's been searching for her missing hatchling the whole time.

## Themes

- Moral ambiguity — is a "victimless" con still a con worth punishing?
- Found family and parental love, seen through a dragon's eyes
- Reputation vs. reality — the gap between what a threat is rumored to be and what it actually is
- Consequences of deception, for both the deceiver and the deceived
- Community trust and how quickly it curdles into fear
- Lighthearted, comedic tone in Acts 1–2 giving way to genuine tension in Act 3

## Hooks

- **Primary — Guild contract:** Elder Maribel Thorne of Thistlewick has written directly to the guild asking for help ending the tribute demands of "Vhalzareth the Undying."
- **Alternative — Caravan escort:** A merchant caravan due through the region has rerouted around Thistlewick entirely rather than risk the "dragon's territory," and the guild is asked to clear the road as a secondary favor while investigating.
- **Alternative — Auction scouting:** A guild broker, hearing rumors of an "ancient dragon's hoard," asks the party to quietly assess whether there's anything worth flagging for a future guild auction — a hook that quietly sets up the disappointment of finding out how thin the "hoard" really is.

## Possible Endings

- **Full Victory:** The party proves Nix is safe and unharmed (whether by exposing him gently, protecting him, or brokering an honest arrangement between him, the village, and his mother). Ignathra collects her son and departs peacefully — potentially even permitting Thistlewick's arrangement with Nix to continue on fairer, more honest terms if the party negotiates it.
- **Partial Victory:** Ignathra is satisfied enough to leave without violence, but cold and suspicious — she takes Nix and warns that she'll be watching Thistlewick from now on. The village keeps its harvest but loses its innocence about how close it came to real danger.
- **Failure / Pyrrhic:** The negotiation with Ignathra collapses — Nix's fate is unknown, he was harmed, or the party is caught misrepresenting the situation — and she attacks Thistlewick. The party survives (retreat and cover are always available), but the village suffers real damage and Nix's fate hangs unresolved.

## DM Notes

The intended emotional arc is comedic-to-tense-to-morally-complex: Acts 1 and 2 should play broad and a little silly (zealous kobolds who've never seen their "god," a hoard that's mostly grain sacks, a dragon's booming voice with a fractional lag), while the fire-breath reveal in Act 2 Scene 2 is the tonal hinge — real danger, real damage, but survivable and revealing rather than lethal. Act 3 should land as a genuine escalation: Ignathra is not a joke, and the party should feel that weight even though the encounter is built to be winnable through conversation.

**Common derailments:**
- **Party attacks Nix on sight, before any investigation:** Fine — this just triggers the Fire Breath/reveal early. Don't let the party kill him outright in one exchange; he flees or begs once his illusion breaks and he's clearly overmatched, per the table's no-surprise-TPK standard.
- **Party decides to kill Nix after the reveal:** This is a valid but heavier path — it locks the story into the Act 3 Failure/Pyrrhic branch, since Ignathra arrives to find her son dead or missing rather than safe. Let the consequences land, but don't ambush the players with an unwinnable fight — the retreat options in [[Ignathra's Return]] still apply.
- **Party can't decide between exposing Nix and protecting him:** That's the point — let them sit with it. Thistlewick and Nix both survive either choice; only the tone of Act 3 changes.

Track internally which way the party leaned by the end of Act 2 (expose vs. protect vs. undecided) — it's the single input that determines how [[Ignathra's Return]] opens.
