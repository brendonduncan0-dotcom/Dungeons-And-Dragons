# D&D Campaign 1 - Sunday Adventurers League

## Campaign Parameters

- **Campaign number:** 1
- **Party size:** 5 players total (rotating DM — 1 player always runs as Dungeon Master, 3–4 remaining players are active Player Characters each session)
- **Character level:** 3 (update as the campaign progresses)
- **Difficulty:** Medium — encounters are designed to drain resources (spell slots, consumables, limited-use abilities) rather than maximise lethality. Death is on the table for poor decisions but must always be clearly telegraphed in advance. Surprise instakills and unavoidable TPKs are off the table.
- **Setting:** Forgotten Realms. The campaign is anchored to the guild headquarters (see World & Lore).

## Theme & Tone

Lighthearted episodic adventure. Tone sits close to a heist comedy or tavern-brawl romp — competent heroes who don't take themselves too seriously, banter between party members, colourful NPCs, and problems that can usually be solved with creativity as readily as swords. Dark moments are fine as contrast but should resolve toward hope or humour. Pacing target: **50% combat / 30% roleplay / 20% exploration**. Each session should feel complete on its own — a self-contained job with a clear beginning, middle, and end.

## Always Include

- **Guild contract framing:** Every session opens with the party receiving a contract from the guild. The hook must include the client or guild officer issuing the job, the stated objective, and the contracted reward. Rewards are agreed upfront and paid on completion.
- **Resource pressure:** Design encounters to tax spell slots, consumables, and limited-use abilities. Multiple smaller challenges per session are preferable to one large spike. The goal is a party that feels spent by the end of a session, not a party that nearly died in round one.
- **Clear danger signals** before any encounter with meaningful death risk: fleeing enemies, environmental scale, NPC warnings, an explicit "you could walk away" beat. Players must always have a meaningful choice to engage or avoid.
- **At least one social encounter** that can be fully resolved without combat — negotiation, deception, persuasion, or performance are all valid.
- **A moment of levity** — a funny NPC, an absurd complication, or a situation the party can play for laughs. Do not make every beat tense.
- **A discoverable secret or bonus objective** that rewards thorough players — hidden loot, a side story thread, or information that adds colour to the world.
- **Encounter variety:** at least one non-combat challenge (trap, puzzle, environmental obstacle, skill challenge) per session.

## Never Include

- Encounters or traps that kill a PC with no warning and no opportunity to react (no save-or-die on first contact, no one-shot from full HP without prior telegraphing)
- **Intentional betrayal in either direction** — the guild does not betray the players, and no story should put the players in a position where betraying the guild is the intended outcome. Guild complications must come from clients or external parties, never from the guild itself.
- Horror involving children or harm to minors
- Sexual content
- Graphic torture as entertainment

## Prefer to Avoid

- Fetch quests with no narrative payoff or NPC personality attached
- Puzzles that require out-of-game knowledge (pop culture, real-world trivia)
- Extended dungeon crawls with no roleplay beats — break up combat rooms with discovery moments
- **Strong mechanical penalties** (permanent stat loss, long-term mechanical disadvantages) — roleplay-based consequences and setbacks are welcome, but avoid punishments that significantly impair a character's mechanical effectiveness for extended periods

## Rewards

- **Primary:** Gold and consumables (potions, ammunition, spell components, etc.)
- **Magic items:** Distributed via occasional in-guild auctions. Magic items are not standard contract rewards — do not include them as session loot. Auction supply is handled separately.
- Contracts always state the gold reward upfront. Bonus rewards for exceeding objectives are appropriate.

## Death & Resurrection

### Death Insurance
The guild's Wizard Shop offers a **retainer-based death insurance** service. Characters pay an ongoing retainer to maintain coverage; if payments lapse, coverage is void. Players may take out this insurance without knowing whether they will ever need it. Details of the service (cost, terms, limits) are to be established in early sessions.

### Resurrection
Resurrection services are available but carry meaningful cost:
- Resurrection comes with **roleplay-based penalties** (trauma, visions, changed outlook, obligations) and/or a **small chance of outright failure** — keeping death something the party genuinely wants to avoid.
- The specific mechanics and failure chance are to be set by the DM running the relevant session.

### Curse & Disease Removal
Removing curses or diseases through the guild's Wizard Shop or Potion Shop is not a simple gold transaction. When a character needs this service, it **triggers a dedicated one-shot** in which the party collects the required materials. The service is then provided on successful completion.

## Penalties

Roleplay-based consequences (reputation damage, NPC attitudes, obligations, restrictions on guild rank advancement) are welcome and encouraged. Avoid imposing strong mechanical disadvantages (persistent debuffs, stat penalties, loss of class features) as punishments.

## Guild Progression

As the party accumulates successful contracts and reputation, they advance through guild ranks. Higher ranks unlock:
- Access to more significant and better-paying contracts
- More influential contacts within the guild and its network
- Additional services or perquisites at the guild headquarters

Rank advancement should feel earned and meaningful. The DM should tie rank milestones to session achievements rather than a fixed XP threshold.

## World & Lore

### The Guild Headquarters

The guild headquarters is a large self-sufficient castle complex — think a working institution with everything its members could need under one roof. It functions as the campaign's home base, social hub, and persistent world anchor.

**Confirmed services:**

| Service | Details |
|---|---|
| General Store | Everyday adventuring supplies, rations, rope, torches, tools |
| Potion Shop | Potions, alchemical items; also provides **disease removal** (requires materials one-shot) |
| Blacksmith | Armour repair, non-magical weapon and armour crafting |
| Weapon Shop | Standard and martial weapons for purchase |
| Tavern | Food, drinks, rumours, downtime socialising |
| Wizard Shop | Arcane supplies; **curse removal** (requires materials one-shot); **retainer-based death insurance** |

The castle also houses member quarters, training facilities, and guild administrative offices. The internal structure (houses, classes, other amenities) is to be developed over early sessions as the party explores the headquarters.
