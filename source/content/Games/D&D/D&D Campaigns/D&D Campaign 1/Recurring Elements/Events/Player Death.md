# Player Death

This note defines the procedure when a player character dies. The DM running the session is responsible for applying this consistently.

## When Death Occurs

A character reaches 0 HP and fails their death saving throws, or is killed by an effect that causes instant death.

**Before running this procedure:** Confirm whether the death was appropriately telegraphed. Per campaign parameters, any encounter with a meaningful death risk must have given the party a clear opportunity to avoid or retreat. If the death was a surprise with no prior warning, treat it as a DM error and discuss with the group before applying permanent consequences.

## Step 1 — Check Death Insurance

Ask the player: **Is your character's death insurance active?**

Death insurance is a retainer service purchased from the [[The Guild Headquarters|Wizard Shop]]. Coverage is only valid if:
- The character enrolled in the service
- Payments are current (have not lapsed)

**If coverage is active:** Proceed to Step 2 (Insurance Payout).
**If coverage is inactive or lapsed:** Proceed to Step 3 (Standard Resurrection Options).

## Step 2 — Insurance Payout

The insurance activates automatically. The character is recovered by guild agents and subjected to whatever revival method the Wizard Shop retainer covers (details to be established when the service is first purchased).

The revival is not guaranteed to be clean — proceed to Step 4 (Resurrection Consequences) regardless of the method used.

## Step 3 — Standard Resurrection Options

Without insurance, resurrection requires the party to pursue it actively. Options available through the guild:

| Method                            | Availability                 | Notes                                            |
| --------------------------------- | ---------------------------- | ------------------------------------------------ |
| Wizard Shop retainer (post-death) | Available, but costly        | Full price without prior insurance; DM sets cost |
| External resurrection services    | Requires travel and gold     | Clerics, temples, powerful NPCs                  |
| Party resources                   | If a party member has access | Revivify window is 1 minute — likely expired     |

The party may choose not to pursue resurrection. A dead character whose player wants to continue can roll a new character, introduced at the DM's discretion.

## Step 4 — Resurrection Consequences

Every resurrection — regardless of method — carries meaningful consequences. The DM determines the specific form based on narrative context, choosing one or more of:

**Roleplay consequences (examples):**
- A vision, haunting memory, or changed outlook from the near-death experience
- An obligation to the entity or service that facilitated the revival
- Shifted relationships with other characters or factions
- A period of physical or mental recovery with narrative effects

**Failure chance:**
- There is a small chance any resurrection simply does not work. The DM sets this probability (suggested: 10–15%) and rolls privately.
- If the resurrection fails, inform the player privately and run the failure moment in-session at the most dramatically appropriate time.

> [!warning] Failure Handling
> If resurrection fails, treat the character as permanently dead. Work with the player to introduce a new character. Do not re-roll — a failed resurrection is final.

## Step 5 — Update Campaign Records

After the session:
- Update the player character note in `Player Characters/` to reflect death or recovery status
- If the character died permanently, note the circumstances in the campaign index under Known Factions/Lore
- If consequences were assigned, note them in the character note for future DMs to reference

> [!tip] DM Note
> Death should feel meaningful without feeling punitive. The goal is that players care about survival without dreading the table. Lean on roleplay consequences over mechanical ones — a character haunted by what they saw is more interesting than a character with -1 to all rolls for three sessions.
